## E1 — the gate now locates the floor itself

### The root cause, stated once
Three rounds, one bug: **the gate trusted metadata supplied by the thing it was testing.**

| | what the producer controlled | how it was defeated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| round 0 (D1) | **whether** `floorMaterial` existed | drop the field, the check skips itself |
| round 1 (E1) | **where** the gate sampled, via `floorRect` | paint the floor, point the rect at a wall |
| now | **nothing** | — |

### The design I chose, and why it cannot be defeated by producer metadata

**`floorRect` is not read at all. `--floor-rect` is gone. The gate segments the image and derives
the ground plane from the pixels.**

I considered the brief's three options and took the one that survives my lane:

* *Producer emits a floor MASK* — rejected: `web/src/export/roomRenders.ts` and `web/src/three/**`
  are Agent N's lane, so I cannot add a first-class artifact. It would also still be metadata to
  validate rather than a measurement to make.
* *Re-project the floor plane from camera pose + room polygon* — **not available**: I checked, and
  the ground truth carries **no room geometry at all** (`rooms[]` is roomId/name/areaM2/materials;
  `walls` is length totals; there is no polygon and no rect anywhere in `out/ground-truth.json`).
  The camera pose lives only in `out/renders/manifest.json`, which is producer metadata of exactly
  the kind E1 says not to trust, and adding geometry to the ground truth means editing
  `web/src/export/takeoff.ts` — Agent O's lane.
* *Compute purity in the gate, from the image* — **taken, and generalised**: instead of measuring a
  crop the producer names, the gate finds every candidate floor surface itself and measures all of
  them.

**The mechanism.** `ground_regions()` runs a gradient-limited flood fill over a 384-px-wide
downsample. **Every seed is on the BOTTOM ROW of the frame** — the one place an interior camera at
1.6 m eye height is always looking at the ground — and a region grows only across gentle shading
(≤ `EDGE_TOL` = 9 levels per channel between *adjacent* pixels), so it stops dead at a material
boundary. Regions above `SEG_Y_MIN` = 0.45 are cut off (the horizon of an eye-height camera in a
2.7–3.0 m room sits near mid-frame, so anything higher has leaked onto a wall). Regions under
`COMP_MIN_AREA` = 0.4 % of frame are specks.

**Why the exploit cannot come back.** A surface that is not connected to the bottom edge of the
frame is unreachable, whatever any manifest says. The Judge's exploit paints the bottom 34 % — so
after painting, *the only regions the gate can reach are the paint*. There is no rect, no crop, no
field and no flag through which the artifact can move the gate's attention off the paint and onto
the untouched upper frame. Falsification I below proves the independence directly: rewriting every
`floorRect` onto a wall, and deleting the field entirely, produce **byte-identical gate output**.

**Three tiers, all decided by the gate:**

| tier | rule | scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A tamper** | every ground surface ≥ 1 % of frame must be **some** material `palette.json` specifies (hue ±10°, sat ±0.10, luminance free to fall) | every render, hard fail |
| **B wrong floor** | a still that shows *none* of its declared finish must not be showing the **other** floor family instead | every render, hard fail |
| **C evidence** | the declared finish must be found on the ground plane at ≥ `EVIDENCE_MIN_AREA` = 2 % of frame | counted, **≥ 3 required** |

Tier A replaced round 1's per-render shadow tier and is strictly stronger for the thing that
matters: round 1 asked "is the producer's crop the declared material, allowing shadow?", which is
answerable only about a crop the producer chose. Tier A asks "is everything the camera sees at the
bottom of this frame a material this design actually specifies?", which is answerable from pixels
alone. The palette envelopes are wide and overlap heavily, so *belongs to nothing* is a strong
signal — and it is exactly what paint, a composite or a substituted texture looks like.

Furniture standing on the floor is the one legitimate reason to fail C — that is what the ≥ 3 bar
exists for — and it cannot hide a repaint, because a repaint fails A and a substituted finish fails
B.

**Thresholds are measured, not tuned.** A sensitivity sweep over `EDGE_TOL` ∈ {6, 9, 12} ×
`SEG_WIDTH` ∈ {320, 384, 480} moved the matched-floor area by under 1 pt on every render
(Reception 5.1–5.9 %, Open_space 31–34 %, Work_stations 42–47 %); at `EDGE_TOL` = 16 Reception
collapses to 0, so 9 sits in the middle of a wide stable plateau. The 2 % evidence bar is 2.5–11×
below what the passing renders measure.

### One variant I did NOT close, and why I think it is correct behaviour

`[G magenta only inside the declared floorRect]` **passes** the new gate (it failed round 1's). This
is honest and, I argue, right. Round 1's gate failed it because the paint landed exactly where the
gate was told to look — a self-inflicted wound, not an exploit. The new gate looks at the ground it
finds; in variant G the paint band does not touch the bottom edge, so the strip of genuine floor
below it still seeds the fill, and the gate reports what is true of that forged pack: Open_space
still shows 7.4 % of frame of its own carpet at the bottom of frame, Work_stations 13.7 % (both down
from 32.1 % / 43.9 %, so the gate *does* see the damage — it just does not call a still floor-less
when the floor is right there). Failing it would require asserting "no pixel anywhere may be an
unexpected colour", which I tried and rejected: measured, it flags the **monitor screens** in
Open_space and Work_stations (`#1C1F22` H=210 L=0.12, `#092B3B` H=199 L=0.14) because `palette.json`
is qbiq's *reference* palette, not an inventory of everything our renderer draws. A gate that goes
red on a monitor gets switched off. Tier A is therefore scoped to the ground plane, where the
semantics actually are, and skips crushed shadow / blown highlight (L outside 0.15–0.93) where hue
and saturation are meaningless.

## E5 / G9 staleness — **both** fixes, because regenerating is cheap

The brief allowed a choice. I measured first: `node scripts/export-pack.mjs` builds **all three
cases in 1.3 s wall clock** (esbuild + one headless Chromium + the wasm core), against a suite that
takes minutes. There is no reason to trade correctness for that, so I did both.

**1 — the runner produces them.** `run-all.sh` gained a **step 0** that runs `export-pack.mjs`
before G10, so the twelve files G9 grades are always written by the generator in the tree, not by
"a 27-minute-old, unrelated invocation". It runs only when G9 is selected (`run-all.sh G6` does not
touch `out/`, verified), and a non-zero exit fails the suite.

**2 — the runner watches them.** The twelve case files joined the closing integrity snapshot
(`CASE_FILES`, diffed exactly like `PACK_FILES`), and the scoreboard now names them:

```
  graded pack: 10/10 artifacts in out/
               + 12/12 G9 round-trip case files, written 18:05:28
               walkthrough.mp4  55022252 B  mtime 18:09:02  43.00s
               unchanged since G10 produced it; PASS  (12 checks)
```

**3 — the gate refuses staleness on its own.** A runner-only fix would leave `python3
g9-roundtrip.py` grading whatever is on disk. G9 now computes the generator's **import closure** and
fails if any case artifact is older than the newest file in it. The closure is *walked*, not listed:
from the four modules `export-pack.mjs` / `demo-doc.mjs` actually bundle
(`qtoWorkbook`, `wallTypes`, `import/dxf`, `import/testfit`) plus the drivers, sample plan, DWG
sample and `web/src/wasm/`. Measured: **64 files**. This precision is deliberate — a blanket
"anything under `web/src`" would call every workbook stale the moment a camera moved, and a guard
that cries wolf gets switched off. The walk reaches `three/sectionRender.ts` (the workbook really
does embed a section render) and does **not** reach `export/roomRenders.ts`,
`export/walkthrough.ts` or `three/interiorStill.ts`, which are the still/video entry points.

Falsification — the exact E5 scenario, "change the generator, run the suite":
