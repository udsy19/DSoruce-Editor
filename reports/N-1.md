## Measurement

I reproduce the Judge's script rather than approximating it: `flat%` = share of 16×16 tiles with
luminance σ < 0.01 on a **960 px-wide BILINEAR** downsample, `edge%` = share of pixels with
`np.gradient` magnitude > 0.02, `eye%` = `flat%` over rows 40–78 %, colours = distinct RGB after
`//8`. **Run against the four reference stills it returns 21.2 / 25.5 / 19.5 / 21.6 and edge

calibrated against both the Judge's control and their round-2 reading.

## 1. E2 — `Conference_room`: the mullion is gone, and the camera is in the room

### What was wrong

Room 39 is `x 5.0–10.0, y 0.1–4.1`. The camera stood at world **(7.50, 5.30)** — 1.25 m **outside**
it, in the corridor — and shot through the room's own glass front. The `GLAZING_MODULE = 1.2` mullion
at x = 7.45 was 0.13 m off the view axis at 1.25 m range, so it ran floor-to-ceiling down the middle
of the frame and through the conference table.

The root cause was not the mullion. It was that "stand in the room" was a **weighted term**
(`INSIDE_BONUS = 1.5`) in a score the corridor camera beat by more than the bonus. Debug dump of the
old scorer, meeting room, wide framing:

```
inside  corner(6.0,1.1) = 6.57      (the only inside candidate that survived)
outside corner(7.5,5.3) = 9.02      <- shipped; 6.57 + 1.5 = 8.07 loses by 0.95
```

### What I changed

`interiorStill.placeRoomCamera` now ranks **lexicographically**: inside-the-room is a *tier*, not a
term. An outside candidate can only win when no camera can stand in the room at all. The candidate
grid is also unconditional now (it used to be generated only for `close` framing or rooms > 12 m), and
the corner inset is tied to the clearance test — `clamp(max(clearance + 0.15, min(w,h) * 0.2), 0.6,
1.1)` instead of a flat 0.75–1.1 m. In a 5 × 4 m room holding a 2.9 m table a metre of inset spends
the only depth the room has.

### Result

| `Conference_room` | before | **after** | reference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| camera | (7.50, 5.30) — **1.25 m outside** | **(5.80, 0.90) — inside** | — |
| mullion through the table | **yes** | **no** | no |
| flat% | 68.1 | **55.9** | 21.6 |
| eye% | 69.2 | **51.8** | 9.9 |
| edge% | 5.9 | **6.9** | 35.4 |
| colours | 329 | **649** | 2510 |
| pixels > 225 on all channels | **23.1 %** | **4.0 %** | 5.1 % |

I looked at the still at full resolution: table receding diagonally with all eight of K's chairs, the
oak door and the perforated-bronze screen behind the glazed front, the linear pendant across the top.
No post anywhere in frame. The blow-out is gone — 23.1 % → 4.0 % is the single biggest number in this
report and it is entirely "the camera is no longer aimed through a lit glass wall".

**In the video too.** `t = 4.2 s` was the same shot. It is now a corridor perspective down the glazed
line to the horizon, with the meeting-room table reading through the glass at the frame edge, not
bisected as the subject: **58.9 → 52.7 flat, 72.6 → 55.3 eye**. I looked at the frame.

## 2. E3 — `Reception`: composed *and* still evidencing its floor

### Why the regression happened, precisely

J's `FLOOR_EVIDENCE_MIN` made "how much floor is in the lower frame" a **rank above composition**.
That rank is decided by a 20-ray probe. On the Reception the two candidate takes measured:

```
corner(1.6,1.6) straight  score 13.98  floorProbes 0.15   <- rank 3, shipped: 46 % of frame is one wall
corner(1.6,1.6) composed  score 15.69  floorProbes 0.10   <- rank 2, discarded
```

The discarded frame's **rendered floor crop measured 100 % pure**. The rank threw away a better
picture on a 1-probe-in-20 difference in a proxy, to protect a check the actual shot passed anyway.

### What I changed

Three things, in order of how much they mattered:

1. **The evidence question is now asked of the rendered floor mask, not of a 20-ray guess.**
   `roomRenders.renderRoomPack` shoots the best-composed camera; only if the developed still genuinely
   cannot evidence its finish does it come back with `floorEvidence: 'required'` (the old rank) and
   pay for it, and only then does it try the alternative framing. **Three of four rooms never reach
   that branch.** `FLOOR_EVIDENCE_MIN` stays at **0.15** — I tried 0.08 and reverted it: at 0.08 the
   proxy started admitting *worse* shots to the top tier and flipped the meeting room to the corner
   that shows two bare walls.
2. **A composition term, from the walkthrough's own scorer.** Every candidate is now scored on
   `frameLook(grid, …, 0).interest` and `.blankFraction` — the mean and the blank share of the frame
   it would actually deliver, over the same `rayInterest` field the video biases every frame with.
   The old score had no term at all for "how much of this frame is plasterboard".
3. **A second candidate heading per standing point**, chosen by `bestVista` inside a ±25° cone, with
   the aim dropped up to 0.25 m as it swings (turning off a near wall swings the room's own floor
   toward the frame edge; tilting down by the same argument keeps it). This is the *hard* argmax, not
   `frameLook`'s soft one — the soft form's turn penalty is a frame-to-frame continuity cost and a
   still has no next frame. Measured on the Reception: soft gives up at +12°, the best frame available
   is at +26°. Both headings are re-scored from scratch, so a swing that loses the room loses.

### Result

| `Reception` | round 1 | round 2 (before) | **after** | reference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| flat% | 48.8 | 57.7 | **43.4** | 21.2 |
| eye% | — | 70.6 | **57.9** | 11.4 |
| edge% | 10.1 | 15.1 | **17.5** | 31.9 |
| colours | 674 | 663 | **737** | 1887 |
| **left 46 % of frame: flat% / mean L** | — | **80.8 / 0.740** | **50.8 / 0.641** | — |
| floor evidenced (G6) | — | yes | **yes** | — |

The Judge measured that left band at 88.9 % (a different tiling); my ruler reads the same file at
80.8 % and reads the new one at **50.8 %**. Leftmost quarter of frame: 88.1 % → 60.6 % flat.

The picture is now the reception desk running into the near foreground, the fluted-oak door and slat
wall, the glazed front with the workstation floor and its perimeter glazing beyond, and the room's own
porcelain/parquet tile bottom-right — which is what G6 samples (`#60462E H=28.8 S=0.35 L=0.28`, inside
the envelope). **It is better than round 1's still as well as round 2's**, which is the bar E3 set.

The open-plan pair needed two more camera changes, both about **depth layering**, which the brief
named and which the plan-space scorer is structurally blind to (the field knows a ray crosses a desk;
it never knows how far away that desk is):

* **A near-field term.** The lower-frame probe pass now reports, in the same 20 raycasts it already
  spent on the floor, what share of the lower frame lands on furniture within 7 m. Rewarded (capped at
  half the band: past that the near desk stops layering the picture and starts being it). This needed
  one line in `materialTheme` — furniture meshes are now marked `userData.furniture`, because nothing
  in the scene graph distinguished a desk 2 m away from a wall 2 m away.
* **The wide framing's stand-back distance is capped at 14 m** (it was `diag * 0.8`). On a 38 m open
  floor that asked the camera to stand 30 m back, which on this plate means the far corner, and every
  metre past about fifteen is bare carpet between the lens and the desks. This alone moved
  `Open_space` from 65.5 to 46.7 in the iteration where I changed it.
* **Two stills of one room must be two pictures.** `Open_space` and `Work_stations` legitimately
  resolve to the same open floor (the reference's do too), and with the scorer improved they converged
  on *literally the same camera*. `RoomPackOpts` now carries the eyes already used for a room and the
  search rejects anything within 9 m of one.

### Honest verdict, per still

* **`Work_stations` (36.4 / 16.3 / 767)** — the best of the four and the closest in kind to its

* **`Reception` (43.4 / 17.5 / 737)** — improved on every axis and better than round 1, but it is

## 4. E6 — the video's opening

### What was wrong

`planWalkRoute`'s first stop aimed at the reception's centroid with `lookWeight 0.9`, i.e. it
authored the opening and then left the composition bias one tenth of the yaw — forbidding, on the
opening, the machinery that fixed the tail. From 2.7 m a 70° frame on a 4 × 3 m glazed box is the sage
wall and, in the right third, the blank flank of the room next door.

### What I changed

The opening beat is now chosen the same way L's closing beat is: **`bestVista` over the shared
scorer**, but inside a **±55° cone around the reception** so the take still *opens on the reception*
rather than on whatever is richest in the building. `lookWeight` 0.9 → **0.55**, span 5.5 → 5.0, so the
per-frame bias has half the yaw to trim with.

### The one thing I did NOT fix, with its number

not earn its place.** No heading helps: in a 2 m corridor the two near walls are the left and right

`t = 22.2` is the other weak frame: **57.3 % flat, 39.9 % of pixels blown**, and it is the core's

well inside G7's 0.85 ceiling, and it was not one of my four defects, so I left it and am reporting it
rather than quietly absorbing it.

## 5. The shared module — what was extracted, and why it is not a third scorer

The brief asked for reuse, not a third scorer. `web/src/export/composition.ts` is **moved code, not
new code**: `ClearanceGrid`, `CELL_*`, `buildClearanceGrid`, `distanceTransform`, `distToPlate`,
`clearanceAt`, `rayInterest`, `bestVista`, `frameLook` all came out of `walkthrough.ts` verbatim.
`walkthrough.ts` is 436 lines shorter and imports them back; `interiorStill.ts` imports the same ones.
It could not live in either consumer — `walkthrough.ts` already imports `EYE_HEIGHT` from
`interiorStill.ts`, so the reverse import would be a cycle.

Two additions, both one-liners on the moved code rather than parallel implementations:

* `FrameLook.interest` — the mean of the fan `frameLook` already computes, so the still can *measure*
  a frame with the identical call the video *biases* with (`maxBias = 0` does no turning at all).
* `bestVista(..., within?)` — an optional cone. The opening beat and the still's alternative heading
  both need "the best heading that still frames my subject"; without it they would each have grown
  their own search.

Deleted in the same change: `INSIDE_BONUS` (superseded by the tier), and the re-export shim I first
added on `walkthrough.ts` — I grepped, nothing outside imported those symbols from it, so the shim was
dead the moment I wrote it.

`renderWalkthrough(state, opts)` and `renderRoomPack(state, opts)` are unchanged in shape;
`deliverablePack.ts` is unaffected. New `CameraPlacementOpts` fields (`grid`, `avoidEyes`,
`floorEvidence`) are all optional.
