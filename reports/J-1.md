## D1 — the artifact can no longer decide whether it is tested

### What was wrong
`g6-renders.py:92-94` skipped its only render-to-model assertion when `ground-truth.renders[].floorMaterial`
was absent — and the producer dropped that field whenever it could not find a clean floor patch. It also
compared against **qbiq's** vocabulary (`"Carpet Light Gray"`) via a hand-written `MATERIAL_KEY` table,
not against our own Inventory strings (`"Carpet tile 500×500 (CPT)"`).

### What I changed

**`web/src/export/roomRenders.ts` (producer)**
- `floorMaterial` is now `FINISH_SPEC[room.finishKey].floor` — literally the string `qtoWorkbook`
  writes into the Inventory `Floor Material` column and into `ground-truth.rooms[].floorMaterial`.
  One vocabulary for plan, workbook and renders.
- `roomRenderGroundTruth` emits `floorMaterial` **unconditionally**. The old
  `...(r.floorCheck === 'ok' ? {…} : {})` was the exploit; it is gone.
- A still that cannot see its floor is **reframed, not excused**: the shot is retaken with the other
  framing and the retake is kept only if it actually evidences the floor (`floorCheckOf(retry) === 'ok'`).
- New `RoomRender.framing` records which framing produced the published still.

**`web/src/three/interiorStill.ts` (camera)** — new `FLOOR_EVIDENCE_MIN`. A camera that can see the
room's own floor now beats one that cannot, *before* score. `INSIDE_BONUS` alone was not enough:
for Reception every corridor camera outscored every camera standing in the room, so the only floor in
frame was behind glass, where the mask cannot reach it. Cameras that already evidenced their floor are
unaffected (they were the max over a superset, so they are still the max over the subset) — measured:
only Reception's camera moved.

**`scripts/gates/g6-renders.py` (gate)** — rewritten around three tiers, all decided by the gate:
| tier | rule | scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity | `roomId` must exist in `ground-truth.rooms[]`; `floorMaterial` must be present; it must **equal** that room's Inventory floor material; `floorRect` must be a plausible lower-frame crop (`y0 ≥ 0.30`, ≥ 0.5 % of frame) | **every** render, hard fail |
| evidence | the crop's median colour is inside that material's `palette.json` envelope | counted, **≥ 3 required** |
| sanity | a render that misses its envelope must still be that material **under shadow** — hue ±10°, sat ±0.10, luminance free to fall (shadow darkens, it does not repaint) | **every** render, hard fail |

The `renders` block itself is now mandatory (dropping it fails), and `MATERIAL_KEY` is deleted — the
palette family is derived by the same rule `materialTheme.floorKeyForFinish` uses (`carpet` → carpet,
otherwise parquet), so a new finish in `FINISH_SPEC` resolves on its own.

### The ≥3 bar — the decision, and why
Reception was fixed by repositioning (it now shoots from inside the room and evidences porcelain/parquet
at H 20.9 S 0.31 L 0.27). **Conference_room cannot be fixed that way**, and I proved it rather than
assuming: with Agent K's meeting-room chairs landed, a 5 × 4 m room with a 2.9 m table and eight chairs
offers **no** clean floor patch from any validated camera. Measured, over the whole interior:

```

eye(7.5,3.3) 0.000 · (9.0,0.9) 0.008 · (9.0,2.1) 0.442 · (9.0,3.3) 0.619
```

I tried relaxing the crop purity to 0.90 and shrinking the search grid to admit small patches: it did
**not** rescue this room (the only floor it can show is in the table's shadow at L 0.26–0.39, under the

pass. **Both relaxations were reverted.** So: the mission's ≥3 bar is enforced explicitly
(`--min-evidenced`, default 3), the miss is printed in full, and tier 3 makes sure the miss is still
*carpet in shadow* and not a different surface. Today 3 of 4 evidence, with the fourth held to tier 3 —
and any single painted-over floor still fails the board (proved below).

## D2 — the graded pack is now the pack the product emits, and it is whole

### Root cause, fixed at the producer
`scripts/render-walkthrough.mjs` streams ~90 s of ffmpeg output straight into `out/walkthrough.mp4`, so
the file's mtime advanced on the first byte and G10's readiness test ("mtime moved" + "> 100 KB")
fired ~36 s before the video existed.

**`deploy/packStore.ts`** now renders into a private staging directory inside `out/` and `rename()`s the
finished files into place (`rename` replaces atomically; only a directory is cleared first). A partially
written take is never visible under its final name. `scripts/export-pack.mjs` got the same tmp+rename
treatment for the workbook/plan/thumbnails. (`writeArtifact` — the browser POST path — was already atomic.)

**Proof, sampled live during a G10 render:**
```
t=16:21:15  stage=22544432  out=25732241  mtime=15:57:58  ffprobe=43.000000
t=16:21:23  stage=24379440  out=25732241  mtime=15:57:58  ffprobe=43.000000
                     (staging dir disappears — the rename)
t=16:21:32  stage=-         out=25702410  mtime=16:21:26  ffprobe=43.000000
```
While the new take was being written, `out/walkthrough.mp4` was still the **previous complete** take and
decoded cleanly; the instant it changed, it was the new complete take and decoded cleanly. The Judge's
`moov atom not found` window is gone — there is no longer an instant at which the published file is partial.

### G10 asserts completeness, not "was touched"
`scripts/gates/g10-one-action.mjs` now waits for every artifact to be **newer than the click AND a complete
file of its kind**, and asserts the same at the end:
- **mp4** — `ffprobe` must return a duration, and it must be inside `video-spec.json durationRange_s`;
- **xlsx** — `PK` header *and* an end-of-central-directory record (the last thing a zip writer emits);
- **png** — signature *and* an `IEND` chunk;
- **json** — must parse.

`--verify-only` does the same without the browser. Note printed by the gate:
`walkthrough.mp4 43.00s · 24.5 MB · mtime 2026-08-04T23:21:26.631Z`.

### Ordering — what I chose and why
**G10 runs FIRST, G1–G9 grade what it left, and the suite closes with an integrity pass.** Producing last
is what created the defect: G10 is not a reader, it clicks the product's export control and rewrites the
whole of `out/`, so anything graded before it is by definition the previous run. The alternative
(re-run all readers after G10) doubles the suite for the same guarantee.

The closing pass makes "green about files that were later overwritten" impossible rather than merely
unlikely:
1. a `name · size · mtime_ns` snapshot of the ten pack artifacts is taken the moment G10 finishes;
2. it is retaken after G1–G9 and **diffed** — any change fails the suite with the diff printed;
3. `g10 --verify-only` re-asserts that the FINAL bytes still parse.
