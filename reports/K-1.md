## 2. D4 — chairs are drawn and billed, once each

### The glyphs are gone
`drawDesk` ended with a `taskChair(...)` call; `drawTable` ringed itself with `seatAt(...)` seats whose
count came from `clamp(round(longLen / 26px), 2, 4)` — **a screen-pixel pitch**. Nothing in the model
matched it, so nothing could bill it. Both blocks are deleted, exactly as B1-3 §3 specified. `seatAt`
became dead and is deleted; `taskChair` dropped to one caller and is inlined into `drawChair`, whose
`cx`/`cy` parameters were then always 0.

I also removed `drawTable`'s 17 % footprint inset. It existed only to leave a ring for the implied
chairs; with them gone it merely under-drew the table and left the real chairs looking detached. The
top now draws at the component's true extent, so a chair visibly tucks under it (see the thumbnail).

### The seats are real components
`seat_around_table(doc, tx, ty, tw, th, clear)` places `floor(long_side / SEAT_PITCH)` seats along
**each** long side plus **one** head seat at each end — office practice takes a single person at a
table end however wide it is. `SEAT_PITCH = 0.75 m` (Neufert / BCO: 600–750 mm of table edge per
person). Each chair faces the table and tucks `CHAIR_SIZE − CHAIR_PROJECT` under it, the identical
tuck `seat_desk_chairs` already gave a workstation, so all seating in the document is one 0.5 m object
at one size.

Seats are **validated, never forced**: each candidate must lie wholly inside `clear` — a walled room's
inner wall faces, or an open setting's zone rect — and must not collide with a seat already placed at
that table. Long sides are placed before heads so a stubby table drops a *head* seat rather than
capacity. On the three cases none is ever dropped.

**Which tables get seats, and why not the others.** Walled rooms with `RoomFurniture::ConferenceTable`
(Meeting / Team Room / Boardroom) and the open **Collaboration** setting. The other open setting is the
Print Point, whose "table" is a copier console — nobody sits at a printer, so it stays unseated. That
is why the rule keys on the room's identity rather than on the mere presence of a `Table`, and it is
why Print Point 1–2 correctly went from *4 chairs drawn / 0 billed* to **0 drawn / 0 billed**.

## 3. D5 — the decision: **(a), mark the glazing, in the geometry**

### First, a correction to the defect report
The claim that *"the plan renderer emits perimeter windows while the classifier bills zero"* does not
hold as stated. I counted `out/plan.png` myself, before any change:

```
exact  #DCDBEE pixels : 0
within 8 of #DCDBEE   : 1120, all at x=50 and x=989 (the facade centrelines)
colour actually there : (226,213,239)
```

Solving the blend: `0.358 × #AEB6FF + 0.642 × #FFE6E6 = (226,213,239)` — those pixels are the
**anti-aliased half-covered edge** of the `perimeter_wall` stroke over the pink circulation wash. They
land inside the judge's ±8 tolerance by coincidence. There were **zero** true perimeter-window pixels;
plan and takeoff were in fact *agreeing* that the facade was solid.

**The substantive half of D5 stands, and is worse than a colour bug:** both artifacts agreed on
something wrong. Our facade read solid (2,406 hairline px : 12,064 wall px, 1:5) where the reference
reads glazed (25:1), and the takeoff billed 128.00 m of `Perimeter wall` against the reference's 6.01.

### The fix, and why it is truthful
`layout::glaze_facade()` re-cuts each architectural wall the classifier already calls `PerimeterWall`
into three collinear segments — **`FACADE_PIER` solid · glazed band · `FACADE_PIER` solid** — setting
`glazing: true` on the band alone. `FACADE_PIER = 0.6 m` is not a new invention: it is
`web/src/export/wallTypes.ts::CORNER_RETURN`, the module the plan renderer's own fallback has always
drawn. There was already one honest convention for the facade; it simply was not in the model.

Why this is authorship rather than fabrication:

* An office **facade is glazed** — that is the premise of the daylight sub-score this generator already
  optimises against (`DAYLIGHT_REACH_M`). Billing 128 m of blank concrete was the fabricated claim.
* The generator authors the fit-out — partitions, glass fronts, doors, furniture. The facade's glazing
  module is the same class of authorship, applied by the same pass that authors everything else.
* **It never overrides the source.** Walls already carrying `glazing` are skipped, so an importer or a
  user that *does* know where the windows are wins outright. Core / keep-out walls, interior
  partitions and every generated wall are untouched (only walls the classifier already types
  `PerimeterWall` are considered), and a document whose walls do not close into a traced plate is left
  alone entirely rather than guessed at.
* The result independently lands on the reference: **123.20 m glazed / 4.80 m solid** against the
  reference's **125.47 / 6.01**. A convention arrived at from first principles reproducing the
  reference to 2 % is the strongest evidence I have that it is the right convention.

### Why in the geometry rather than in a classifier rule
One fact, not two. `quantity::classify_wall` bills the band as `Perimeter windows` (rule 2) and the
piers as `Perimeter wall`; `wall_types()` hands the plan renderer that *same* classification;
`materialTheme` builds glass there in 3D; `cost.rs` prices it. Plan and workbook cannot drift because
there is only one thing to read. A span-splitting classifier would have needed a parallel convention in
`wallTypes.ts` — precisely the two-conventions problem D5 is about.

It is **idempotent** (an already-glazed band is skipped; a 0.6 m pier is far below `MIN_GLAZED_RUN`),
deterministic, and runs as the *last* statement of `generate()` so the zone/component ids the workbook
uses as Room IDs never move.

## 4. D8 — the real cause was zone bucketing, not the headcount rule

`quantity::headcount` was fine. `Document::zone_index_at` was not:

```rust
} else {
    // Non-circulation is preferred; last one wins.
    chosen = Some(i);
```

The generator emits its rooms **before** the workspace field, and the oriented field is a
plate-spanning rect that **encloses the band rooms**. "Last one wins" therefore handed a cabin's chair
to *Open Workspace*. Cabin 1 kept its chair only because the field rect happened to miss it; Cabins 2
and 3 sat inside it and reported 0. Measured on the untouched tree, seed 7:

```
zone 71  Cabin 1        members 3      zone 139 Cabin 2   members 0
zone 74  Collab         members 0      zone 148 Cabin 3   members 0
zone 115/123/131 Phone Booth 1-3       members 0
zone 153 Open Workspace members 145    <- 166 components, only 21 left in rooms
```

That also explains the *other* half of the symptom the judge saw: the `Furniture Elements` column comes
from `takeoff.ts::zoneAtPoint`, which takes the **first** containing zone (rooms, since they are emitted
first) while `Headcount` came from the **last**. Two different bucketing rules on one row — which is why
a room could print `Chair: 1` next to `Headcount: 0`.

**Fix:** among containing zones prefer non-`Circulation`, and within that class take the
**smallest-area** zone. Order-independent, so identical rooms are bucketed identically by construction.
It is also exactly the ownership rule `layout::conform_zones_to_plate` already uses ("a cell inside one
or more zone rects is owned by the SMALLEST-area one"), so the two passes now agree on who owns a piece
of floor — and it is what makes `zoneAtPoint`'s first-wins result *provably* the same answer rather
than a lucky one. (Follow-up outside my lane: `takeoff.ts::zoneAtPoint` should adopt the same rule so
the agreement stops depending on emission order at all.)

## 6. Existing assertions I refined (and why each is still honest)

Two tests encoded "the facade is exactly the user's N wall segments". `glaze_facade` re-cuts each run
into three, so the segment *count* changes while the geometry does not. Neither refinement weakens what
the test protected:

1. **`generated_rooms_are_enclosed_with_glass_front_and_door`** asserted `non-generated count == 4`. It
   now asserts the stronger, geometric invariant: 12 segments, **summing to exactly the plate perimeter
   `2×(30+20)`**, with exactly one glazed band per run. A wall that moved, vanished or double-counted
   now fails where the bare count would not have.
2. **`regenerate_replaces_generated_walls_and_keeps_user_walls`** asserted "no extra non-generated
   walls appear"; it now asserts `3 × user_ids.len()`, after three generate calls. Its untouched
   sibling assertion (`doc.walls.len()` unchanged across a regenerate) is what proves the pass does not
   accumulate — and it passed without modification.
3. **`quantity::a_generated_testfit_produces_a_coherent_quantity_surface`** hand-authors three facade
   walls glazed and one solid. Expected windows/wall moved 64/16 → **78.8/1.2**: the three authored
   glazed walls are untouched (proving the `!glazing` skip respects the source), and only the wall
   authored *solid* receives the 0.6 m-pier module — 16 − 1.2 = 14.8 m of new glazing. The total
   perimeter assertion (`== 2×(24+16)`) was left alone and still passes, so no length was invented.

## 9. Risks / follow-ups

1. **`takeoff.ts::zoneAtPoint` still takes the FIRST containing zone.** It now returns the same answer
   as the core for every case we ship, but by emission order rather than by rule. One-line fix (prefer
   the smallest containing zone) in a file outside my lane.
2. **Door swings are still not modelled as obstacles.** A head seat is validated against walls,
   components and its neighbours, but not against a leaf's arc. It cannot fire on the current program
   (the nearest head seat sits ≥1.1 m from a hinge whose arc is 0.9 m), but it is not structurally
   impossible on a hand-authored tiny room.
3. **`glaze_facade` splits *architectural* walls.** That is deliberate — it is how the fact gets into
   the one model everything reads — but it means a user's single facade wall becomes three selectable
   segments after a generate. Idempotent and pinned by tests, and the original wall keeps its id (it
   becomes the near pier), so nothing that references a wall id breaks.
4. **Cost / carbon rise honestly** with the 40 new chairs: `cost.rs` prices seating at ₹12,000 and
   55 kgCO₂e each, so the seeded case gains ₹480,000 of indicative capex and 2,200 kgCO₂e — furniture
   that was always drawn and is now finally paid for.
