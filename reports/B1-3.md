# B1-3 — Every generated desk now carries its task chair

**Lane: `crates/ds-core/**` only.** No renderer, viewer, workbook or deploy file touched.

**Headline: the seeded plate went from 63 desks / 9 chairs to 63 desks / 72 chairs.**
Every workstation is seated; the Furniture Inventory gained 63 line items; the open-workspace
headcount stayed 63 (not 126). Gates: **8/10, up from 7/10** — G1–G6 and G9 all still green.

## 1. What I changed

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `crates/ds-core/src/layout.rs` | **`seat_desk_chairs()`** — new post-pass; four `CHAIR_*` constants; called from `generate()` just before `reassign_components()` |
| `crates/ds-core/src/circulation.rs` | **`is_loose_seating()`** — `Chair` no longer blocks the occupancy raster |
| `crates/ds-core/src/quantity.rs` | `headcount()` — a zone with desks is counted by its **desks**, not desks + chairs |
| `crates/ds-core/src/layout.rs` (tests) | `assert_chairs_are_seated()` + 2 new tests; 3 existing assertions refined (§5) |

### The seating rule

One `Chair` (0.5 × 0.5 m, matching the seats `furnish_room` already emits) per non-reference `Desk`.

* **Which side.** A desk's user sits on its local **+y** side — `furniture.ts::drawDesk` puts the
  monitor on the −y back edge and states "the user sits toward +y". The pass tries +y first and −y
  second. That single fallback handles both packing regimes without the pass having to know which
  ran: a bench pair butts its two desks together with `SPINE_GAP == 0`, so +y is solid desk and the
  seat correctly lands in the outer aisle — the only place a person can physically sit in a
  back-to-back run.
* **Orientation.** The chair faces its worktop, following the convention already established by
  `furnish_room` (`ReceptionDesk` seats at `rot`, `WorkPoint` at `rot + π`).
* **Tuck.** The chair overlaps its own desk and projects `CHAIR_PROJECT = 0.35 m` into the aisle,
  capped at `(clear − 0.20) / 2` so two chair backs sharing one `desk_clearance_m` aisle always leave
  0.20 m of walkable floor. With the default 0.9 m clearance the projection is 0.35 m and 0.20 m
  remains between backs.
* **Never forced.** Each candidate is validated against the plate polygon, interior walls, every
  other component and every chair already seated in this pass. Projections are tried at
  0.35 → 0.175 → 0.05 m, so a desk pinned against architecture keeps a fully-tucked seat rather than
  an overlapping one, and a desk with genuinely no room gets none. In practice this never fires:
  across 3 plate shapes × 3 seeds every desk was seated (§4).

### Why a post-pass, not part of the packer

Chairs are decided **after** all desks are placed and are **never** added to the packer's obstacle
list. Desk placement is therefore byte-identical to before this change — desk counts, seed
determinism and every `LayoutScore` term are untouched, which is what let me make a change this
broad with no layout regressions to chase.

### Chairs are not circulation obstacles

`circulation::rasterize_components` now skips `Chair`, the same class of exemption `Door` already
had. A task chair tucks under its worktop and rolls away: it is neither fixed construction nor a
permanently installed furnishing, and ADA 2010 §403 / IBC 1020 measure required clear width to those.

This was **load-bearing, not cosmetic**. Without it, two chair backs in a bench aisle would rasterize
as a 0.20 m chokepoint and `real_plate_...` assertions such as
`min_corridor_width >= 0.95 × desk_clearance_m` would have failed outright. Because the change only
ever *removes* blockers, every circulation assertion in the suite is a lower bound (`score >= 54/55`,
`largest_connected > 0.98`, `min_corridor >= …`) and can only be helped. Measured on the real plate,
seed 7: circulation score **90.7**, min corridor **0.30 m** — unchanged from before, since the only
newly-unblocked pre-existing components were the 7 room chairs.

### Accounting: chairs must not inflate pax

A workstation is a desk **and** its chair, seating one person. `quantity::headcount` previously
counted `Desk + Chair` per zone, which would have doubled every open-plan headcount the moment this
pass landed. It now counts **desks** when the zone has any, and falls back to **chairs** for
desk-free zones (meeting rooms, breakout) where chairs are the only seats. `metrics().workstations`
and `zone_stats().seated` were already desk-only (`is_workstation`) and are unaffected.

Verified on the real exported workbook — the open-workspace Inventory row:

```
room 153  headcount 63  |  Chair W50 X L50: 63, Desk W70 X L140: 63
total headcount across Inventory: 67        (63 open-plan + 4 single-seat rooms)
```

63 desks and 63 chairs in one room reporting a headcount of **63**, not 126.

## 3. Conference tables — what I chose, and why

**I did NOT emit chairs around meeting/conference tables. Desks only.** The orchestrator asked for
judgement here, so this is the reasoning in full, because I think the decision is finely balanced.

The BOM argument *for* doing it is strong: a `Table W190 X L290` boardroom currently bills **0 chairs
and headcount 0**, which is as wrong as the desk case was.

What stopped me is a hard collision with a file outside my lane. `web/src/editor/furniture.ts`
already draws implied seating in the 2D plan glyphs:

* `drawDesk` draws one tucked task chair as part of the desk symbol;
* `drawTable` draws a **ring of chairs around the perimeter**, count and pitch derived from the
  table's on-screen size (`perSide = clamp(round(longLen / 26px), 2, 4)` plus head seats).

For **desks** this is benign: my component chair lands essentially on top of the glyph's implied one
(same side, same tuck, same orientation), so 2D reads as one slightly heavier chair. G4 and G5 both
stayed green.

For **tables** it is not benign. The glyph ring is pitched in *screen pixels*, so it is not
reproducible from Rust; component chairs would land between the drawn ones and the plan would show
visibly doubled seating around every meeting room — a regression to deliverable #2 in exchange for
fixing deliverable #1.

The correct end state is real `Chair` components everywhere **and** `furniture.ts` no longer drawing
implied seats. That second half is a one-file change I am not permitted to make.

**Follow-up for whoever owns `web/src/editor/furniture.ts`** (small and well-specified):

1. Delete the tucked-chair block at the end of `drawDesk` (`const seat = …; taskChair(...)`) — the
   desk's chair is now a real component and is drawn by `drawChair`.
2. Delete the `seatAt` ring and head-chair block from `drawTable`, leaving just the worktop.
3. Ping me and I will add table seating to `seat_desk_chairs` in the same shape as the desk pass
   (perimeter seats on a geometric pitch, validated against the room's inner faces and `TABLE_CLEAR`).

Until then, meeting rooms honestly report `headcount 0` rather than a fabricated seat count.
`RoomQuantity` already carries `capacity` (the area rule-of-thumb) alongside `headcount` if the
workbook would rather show an estimate — that mapping lives in `qtoWorkbook.ts`, also outside my lane.

## 4. The regression test

`every_generated_desk_gets_exactly_one_task_chair` — 3 plate shapes (rectangular single region,
L-plate multi-wing, and the real 843 m² irregular plate that drives the *oriented* packer) × 3 seeds,
asserting:

* a **strict 1:1 matching** — each desk claims its own distinct adjacent chair, so N desks sharing
  one seat cannot pass;
* **no desk is double-seated** — the count of chairs within reach of any desk equals the desk count;
* no chair overlaps another chair;
* every chair is inside the plate;
* `assert_no_overlaps` still holds for everything else;
* **accounting**: for every zone holding desks, `quantity` headcount == that zone's desk count.

`regenerate_does_not_accumulate_chairs` — a clean regenerate re-seats rather than stacks, and a
`keep_confirmed` regenerate over Confirmed desks *and* Confirmed chairs still leaves exactly one seat
per desk (the pass recognises a surviving seat and does not add a second).

## 5. Existing assertions I refined (and why each is still honest)

Three tests encoded "a desk has no chair" implicitly. None of the refinements weakens what the test
was actually protecting:

1. **`assert_no_overlaps`** now exempts `Chair` ↔ `Desk`/`Table`. A tucked chair overlapping its
   worktop is what "seated at the desk" *means* — the 2D glyph tucks it the same way. The exemption
   is deliberately narrow: chair↔chair and worksurface↔worksurface stay strict, and the new
   `assert_chairs_are_seated` independently proves each chair tucks under at most one worksurface, so
   the exemption cannot hide a chair sprawled across two desks.
2. **`every_component_is_bucketed_into_a_zone`** compared the Workspace zone's *member count* to the
   desk count; the zone now also holds each desk's chair. It now counts **desks among the members**,
   which is the invariant the test names ("all desks in workspace").
3. **`keep_confirmed_freezes_components_and_packs_around_them`** asserted nothing overlaps a frozen
   desk; its own seat now does, by design. `Chair` is skipped there with the same rationale.

## 7. Risks

1. **Desk chairs are drawn twice in 2D** — once by my `Chair` component, once by the implied chair
   inside `drawDesk`. They coincide almost exactly (same side, same tuck), so it reads as one heavier
   chair and G4/G5 stayed green, but it is real duplicated ink. Fixed by step 1 of the §3 follow-up.
2. **Meeting rooms still bill 0 chairs and headcount 0** — §3. This is the remaining half of the bug
   the orchestrator described, and it is blocked on a file outside my lane.
3. **`seat_desk_chairs` costs ~1% of generate time** — measured on the real plate over 8 seeds in
   debug: mean 240.2 ms → 242.8 ms (+2.6 ms). Worth stating precisely because
   `real_building_plate_spreads_the_program` asserts a 150 ms debug budget and is the known flaky
   test from B1-1 §6 (it failed 3 of 4 runs on the *untouched* baseline under concurrent agent load;
   it fails ~1 run in 5 now, and passes reliably at `--test-threads=2`). The +1% did not change its
   behaviour, but that budget should become a non-timing assertion.
4. **A chair could in principle be seated over a door swing.** Doors are components and so are in the
   collision set, but the *swing arc* is not modelled. No occurrence across the 9 generate runs the
   new test covers — desks keep a full clearance from partitions — but it is not structurally
   impossible.
