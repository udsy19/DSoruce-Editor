## 1. Root cause — it was the look-ahead, not the dressing

`buildPoses` aimed the camera at a point a **fixed 7.5 m ahead on the path**. On a test-fit plate a
corner is a partition, so for the ~2 s before every turn the camera was pointed squarely into the
blank flank of the room it was about to walk past, with the corridor it was actually standing in
shoved out to the frame edge. That is exactly what produced both flagged moments, and the geometry
says so:

* **t=11 s**, eye at (15.7, 4.9). The partition `(13.6, 6.2)–(16.4, 6.2)` is 1.26 m away and subtends
  bearings 60.6°–149°. The camera was at yaw 59° — mid-turn — so the wall covered rel −3°…+35°, i.e.
  the right 54 % of a 70° frame. Any yaw in [25°, 115°] is dominated by it; only yaw ≤ 43° escapes,
  and yaw ≤ 43° is *down the corridor*, which is where the camera should still have been looking.
* **t=33 s**, eye at (33.7, 9.2). The partition `x = 32.1, y = 6.1…9.3` is 1.56 m away. The camera was
  already yawed 21° past due-south into the turn it had not reached yet.

A dense 41-frame sweep of the baseline (not the 12-frame sample) found the defect was **worse than
reported**: t=30 and t=31 were 82 % and 85 % blank, at frame luminance 0.870 and **0.889 — over G7's
own 0.85 ceiling**. G7 does not sample there, so it shipped green.

## 2. What changed — `web/src/export/walkthrough.ts` only

Three changes, in descending order of how much they bought.

### 2.1 `visibleLookAhead()` — look at the furthest point you can SEE (the fix)

The look-ahead now walks outward from the camera and stops at the last path point with an
unobstructed sight line (`sightClear`, 0.32 m clearance along the segment — under a doorway's own
0.45 m half-width, so a threshold does not read as a wall). In a straight corridor it is still the
full 7.5 m; approaching a corner it collapses to the corner distance, so the frame holds the corridor
until the corner opens and then turns through it. Which is also what a person's head does.

This alone took the worst frame from 84.6 % to 52.6 % and t=11 from 71.9 % to 26.5 %. It **lowered**
the peak yaw rate (45.4 → 42.6 °/s at that stage) rather than whipping: the turn is later but not
faster, because the same ±0.75 s smoother still spreads it.

### 2.2 A composition bias built on the clearance field's own cells

`buildClearanceGrid` already rasterises everything; it now also records **what** each blocked cell is
(`CELL_WALL` / `CELL_GLASS` / `CELL_CORE` / `CELL_CONTENT`) — a perimeter run (whole length on the
plate edge, rendered as spandrel + glazing) and a glazed partition are daylight, not blank surface.
No second rasteriser, no second pass.

`rayInterest()` marches a sight line over that field. **Furniture does not stop it** — an eye at
1.60 m looks over a 0.75 m desk — so a desk bank accumulates as *content* and the ray continues to
the surface behind. A direction scores on depth (0.40), content crossed (0.35) and what it lands on
(0.25). A blank partition at 0.8 m scores 0.02; a desk field ending at the facade scores ~0.9.

`frameLook()` fans 87 rays across the frame plus the bias range, scores every admissible yaw offset
as the mean over the window it would frame minus a quadratic turn penalty, and returns a **soft
argmax** (temperature-weighted mean, not a pick) so the offset varies continuously along the walk.
Capped at ±30°.

Two things this needed to be correct:

* It runs **after** the yaw smoother, not before. Folded into the raw yaw it is averaged away by the
  ±0.75 s kernel and the frame that ships is still the one aimed at the wall — measured: identical
  worst frame. Applied to the smoothed yaw and re-smoothed on its own shorter 0.4 s kernel, it
  corrects the frame that is actually delivered.
* It inherits only the share of the yaw that is **not authored** (`1 − W`). The hero has
  `lookWeight: 1`, so its bias is exactly zero and the branded-screen shot is byte-for-byte the
  composition E-1 built — confirmed: hero eye-band 44.0 → 43.6 %, luminance 0.815 → 0.815.

*Measured and rejected:* a floor under that share (letting authored shots take 30 % of the
correction) changed **nothing** — where an authored shot still carries a blank flank, the bias
already reports no better yaw exists within ±30°. It was removed rather than left in as dead
complexity.

### 2.3 `centreLaterally()` — pass the partition at arm's length

Each path sample slides perpendicular to travel onto the local clearance ridge, capped at 0.8 m,
smoothed over ~1 m of path and tapered to zero at both ends so the authored opening and hero poses do
not move. A wall 0.6 m off the eye fills most of a 70° frame however the camera is pointed. This is
what took t=34 from 50.4 % to 38.3 % and t=14 from 8.6 % to 2.7 % at eye level.

**Its cost, stated plainly:** the route grows 51.48 → 54.96 m, so mean speed goes 1.226 → 1.309 m/s
inside the same 43 s. E-1 already ran above `video-spec`'s 0.8 m/s note (which is a note, not a G7
assertion); this is 7 % more. Revert `maxOffset` to 0.45 for 53.77 m / 1.28 m/s and give back ~3
points of eye-band on t=34 / t=14.

## 3. Evidence — I read the frames as images

**t = 11 s.** Before: the right 72 % of the frame is one featureless white partition; the desks,
chairs, pendants and glazing are crushed into the left edge. After: the open floor fills the frame —
the glazed office block on the left, desk banks with task chairs receding to the perimeter glazing,
the ceiling downlight grid, and the perforated-bronze screen as a near-field anchor on the right
edge. **71.9 % → 20.8 % of frame; 71.9 % → 4.4 % at eye level.**

**t = 33 s.** Before: the left 55–70 % is blank wall, content crammed right. After: the left ~30 % is
that wall, and the remaining 70 % is a corridor of glazed office fronts with a lit ceiling, parquet,
desks and chairs behind the glass and daylight at the end. **71.9 % → 50.2 % of frame; 66.7 % →
41.1 % at eye level.** It now reads as a composed corridor shot with a near-field anchor on one edge
— which is what the reference pack does — rather than as a rendering error.

**t = 30–31 s** (the baseline's true worst, 82 % and 85 %): now 17.9 % and 20.6 %, **0.0 %** at eye
level.

### About the 50.2 % that is left

Two caveats, both measured, neither hidden:

1. **The metric merges the ceiling.** It finds the largest connected near-flat same-colour region. In
   an interior at 1.60 m with pitch locked at 0, a pale wall and the ceiling join through a soft
   lighting gradient and count as one surface. The mask overlay for t=33 shows this directly. The
   eye-level band figure (rows 40–78 % of frame height) is the honest "how much of this is blank
   wall" number: **41.1 %** at t=33.
2. **The two frames still over 50 % at eye level are the reception approach (t=2, t=4)**, which the
   brief did not flag and which the baseline had at 63.6 % and 74.7 %. At **t=2 the flat surface is
   the reception's sage feature wall** (measured rgb 183,182,150 — green, not plasterboard): it is
   the designed identity surface the shot exists to show, and the metric cannot tell sage plaster
   from white board. t=4 is a corridor elevation carrying a black linear pendant, an oak table in the
   near field and daylight at the vanishing point; the bias reports no better yaw exists there within
   ±30°.

## 5. API stability (for Agent H)

`renderWalkthrough(state, opts)` and `WalkthroughOpts` are **unchanged**. The additions are
additive only: `WalkthroughSummary` gains `maxLookBiasDeg` / `worstBlankBefore` / `worstBlankAfter`,
`ClearanceGrid` gains `kind`, and five `CELL_*` constants are exported. Nothing was removed or
renamed. `renderTitleCard`, `buildClearanceGrid`, `routeBetween`, `splineThroughStops`,
`planWalkRoute`, `bestFreePoint`, `clearanceAt` all keep their signatures.

## 7. Open risks (updated from E-1 §6)

1. **Luminance headroom is better, not worse.** The hero still measures 0.815 against the 0.85
   ceiling (0.035 of margin, unchanged by design — the bias is locked out there). The *rest* of the
   take improved: the highest frame luminance anywhere fell from 0.889 to 0.815, so the take no
   longer contains a frame that would fail G7's band if it happened to be sampled. Turning toward
   content turns away from white plasterboard.
2. **1.309 m/s**, up from 1.226 (§2.3). Dial `centreLaterally`'s `maxOffset` down to trade framing
   back for pace.
3. **The reception approach (t=2–4) is now the weakest passage**, at 59–62 % eye-band. It was 64–75 %
   before. The bias reports no better yaw within ±30°, so improving it needs a different opening
   stop, not a different camera — out of scope for this fix and not a defect the brief raised.
4. Everything else in E-1 §6 (door leaves hidden, hardware GL, the driver muxing the mp4,
   `out/walkthrough.json` being unread by any gate) is unchanged.
