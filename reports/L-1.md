## 1. D7 — the ending

### What was actually wrong

The take ended standing **inside a 5 × 4 m meeting room, 0.6 m from two of its walls**
(hero pose `(30.13, 3.42)`, room `x 25.85–30.85 · y 0.1–4.1`, clearance 0.60 m). At 1.6 m eye height
in a 70° frame that geometry has one outcome whatever the camera does: three wall planes and the
ceiling fill it, with a table edge along the bottom. Agent K's chairs — **which had landed before I
rendered anything** (`97 walls · 206 components`, up from 89/166) — put seating in that room and it
did not change the arithmetic: the seats sit below the horizon and the walls above it are still the
frame.

The whole last quarter was the problem, not just the last shot. From t≈32 the route left the open
floor, came down a **1.5 m slot between office 148's flank and the plate edge** (blank white wall
filling the left third at t=34), ran back west along the corridor, and turned into the white box.

### The fix: end where the reference ends

`video-spec.json` records the reference's own last sampled frame:

> `{ "t": 36.8, "lum": 0.637, "scene": "Final open-plan bay, facing the opposite window wall" }`

qbiq **never enters a meeting room** — it arcs *past* the glazed conference room and closes deep in
the open plan. I looked at `frame08`/`frame09` to confirm it. So the route now does the same:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| stops | reception → corridor → open-space → open-space-far → **conference-threshold → conference-hero** | reception → corridor → open-space → open-space-far → **open-hero** |
| route | 54.96 m | **43.68 m** |
| mean speed | 1.309 m/s | **1.04 m/s** (spec note is 0.8; this is the closest the take has been) |
| min clearance | 0.431 m | **0.570 m** |
| containment fixes | 0 | **0** |
| threshold passes | 8 | 7 (spec floor is 3) |
| desk-field run (leg a→b) | 8.7 m | **12.3 m** |

Three changes in `planWalkRoute`, plus one helper:

1. **The hero bay is resolved FIRST and the spine is parameterised to it.** `finish` used to be the
   conference door; it is now `onSpine(heroAt)`, where `heroAt` is `bestFreePoint(preferOpen)` at the
   far end of the open floor, held a fifth of the floor's length off the end wall and ~45 % of its
   depth off the spine. On the demo plate that moved the spine anchor from x = 31.17 to x = 32.38 —
   1.2 m — so the reception, corridor and first open-space stops are where they were and the
   Judge-praised middle of the take is unperturbed.
2. **`bestVista()`** (new, 20 lines, next to `rayInterest`): a full-circle scan of the *same*
   `rayInterest` scorer the per-frame composition bias uses, scored as the sum over the frame each
   heading would deliver. The closing direction is therefore chosen by the same measure of "what is
   worth looking at" that E2 built, not by a hand-picked look target. It could not reuse `frameLook`
   — that fans only ±(hfov/2 + bias) around an existing yaw and there is no existing yaw for a hero
   shot — so it shares the scoring function instead of the search. It chose 129.7° (north-west,
   down the desk field into the perimeter glazing).
3. **`open-space-far` pulled from t = 0.92 → 0.78 on the spine.** At 0.92 it landed 1.3 m from the
   hero and the take finished by walking a metre *backwards* into its own last shot. At 0.78 the
   final approach is a clean 8 m.

### Measured — tail, before vs after

`flat%` = share of 16×16 tiles with luminance σ < 0.01 on a 960 px downsample (D3's definition).
`eye%` = the same on rows 40–78 % of frame height (E2's eye-band, the honest "how much of this is
blank wall" number). **Both columns are from one script run over both files**, so they are
comparable to each other; my absolute `flat%` runs ~10 pts above the Judge's on the same frame
(different resize filter — mean luminance agrees to ±0.001, e.g. 0.814 vs their 0.815 at t≈42).

| last 10 s | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| **worst frame that is one flat surface** | **68.3 %** (t=40.2) | **43.0 %** (t=39.2) |
| worst at eye level | **84.5 %** (t=40.2) | **39.7 %** (t=39.2) |
| worst mean luminance | **0.814** | **0.599** |
| the final frame (t=42.2) | 67.5 % · eye 81.0 % · L 0.814 | **34.0 % · eye 24.7 % · L 0.570** |

Whole take: worst mean luminance **0.814 → 0.782** at 1 s spacing, **0.826 → 0.799** on a dense
0.2 s sweep — G7's ceiling is 0.85, so headroom went from 0.024 to **0.051**. Mean `flat%` across the
43 sampled frames 53.3 % → 47.7 %.

### I read the frames

14 frames at ~3 s spacing, plus every second of the tail. What the ending is now:

* **t = 34.2** — a wide of the deep bay: near-field desk return bottom-right, bench desks and task
  chairs receding left, the full perimeter glazing band with the green horizon, black linear pendants
  in rhythm across the ceiling. 37.0 % flat / 23.1 % eye.
* **t = 39.2** — the tail's flattest frame (43.0 %). It is an aisle opening onto the facade: two desk
  banks framing a floor gap, glazing across the whole width. The "flat" it is measuring is the
  ceiling plus the carpet gap between the two desk runs, not a wall.
* **t = 42.2 (the closing frame)** — desk banks and monitors filling the lower half, the glazing band
  and horizon across the middle, pendants above. It is the same shot the reference closes on, and it
  is now the *strongest* frame in the film rather than the weakest. Compare `t034.20` here with
  `docs/reference/qbiq/spec/video-frames/frame09_t36.8.png`.

For contrast, the shot it replaced (old t=42.2) was: table edge bottom-left, DSOURCE screen left,
**blank plasterboard filling the right half and the top**.

### Two knock-on changes, stated plainly

**a) In-scene branded displays: 3 → 2.** The third hung on the conference room's identity wall. The
take no longer enters that room, and a screen the camera never sees is not branding (the module's own
rule). It could not be moved to a passing shot: the meeting-room doors on this plate sit at the far
corner of the corridor wall, so from the spine the sight line through a 0.9 m opening misses the
identity wall by ~0.2 m — I checked the geometry rather than guessing. So the count is honest: two
in-scene screens plus the title card, against the reference's three in-scene and no card.

**b) The reception screen was half out of frame, and now is not.** `addBrandedDisplays` picked the
reception's identity wall with `facingWall(room, from = the room's own door)`. Shot from the camera's
actual stop that wall is 40° off frame, which is why the opening beat was a bare sage wall. It now
uses the **stop the camera reads the room from**, which is what the code comment already claimed it
did. The mark lands dead centre on the sage feature wall above the oak table: t=2.2 went 69.1 % flat
/ 90.9 % eye → **64.9 % / 80.5 %**, t=3.2 → 47.3 % / 60.0 %.

**c) The core screen is 1.7 m → 1.3 m.** The route brushes the core flank at 1.7 m and a 1.7 m screen
there subtends 53° of a 70° frame — measured **0.821 mean luminance** at the closest frame, i.e. the
old white-wall defect relocated to t=22 rather than removed from the film. At 1.3 m (a 55" wayfinding
screen, not a 75" one) the same pass measures **0.739 / 57.1 % flat**, and it still reads clearly
from the aisle 3 m back. This is a prop dimension, not a filter or a grade.

## 2. D9 — encode

**60 fps, matching the reference exactly.** The cost is real and I measured it rather than estimating:
**1290 → 2580 frames, 101 s → 223 s** of render wall-clock on an M4 Pro. That cost is also paid by
`deploy/packStore.ts`, which invokes the driver with no `--fps`, so the in-app one-action pack's video
stage roughly doubles (~2 min → ~4 min). I took it: the deliverable is a client hand-off, the camera
never stops moving, and half the reference's frame rate is visible as judder in exactly the pans this
take is made of. `--fps 30` still gives the cheap take for iteration.

**CRF 18 → 15.** Bitrate **4.78 → 9.82 Mb/s**; file 24.5 → 52.8 MB. The reference is 33.1 Mb/s, so the
gap closes from ~7× to ~3.4×, and I am not going to claim it should close further: a photoreal render
with foliage and a city beyond the glass carries far more entropy per frame than our synthetic
interior, whose large smooth ceiling gradients are exactly what CRF 15 stops banding. Pushing to
CRF 10 would triple the file to encode noise that is not in the source.

Everything else is unchanged: `-preset slow`, lossless PNG frames into x264, `yuv420p` High profile,
range pinned `full → limited`, `+faststart`, no audio.

## 5. Open risks

1. **The opening is now the weakest passage**, as E2 predicted it would become: t=2.2 is 64.9 % flat /
   80.5 % at eye level, and the take's luminance peak is t≈3.9 at **0.799** (ceiling 0.85). Most of
   that "flat" is the reception's **sage feature wall** — a designed identity surface, measured
   rgb ≈ (183,182,150), which the metric cannot tell from plasterboard — but the white partition
   filling the right third of the frame is real. Fixing it needs a different opening stop, not a
   different camera; out of scope here and it is not what D7 flagged.
2. **`open-space-far` at spine t = 0.78 is tuned on this plate.** On a plan whose open floor is
   shorter it collapses onto the hero again (seed 11 shows 1.8 m). The robust form is to place it a
   fixed arc-length back from the hero rather than at a fixed spine fraction.
3. **The pack render doubles** (§2). If that is unacceptable for the in-app path, `packStore.ts`
   should pass `--fps 30` explicitly rather than the driver defaulting down — the deliverable and the
   in-app export would then differ, which is why I did not do it.
4. `out/walkthrough.mp4` is still written by more than one agent (INCIDENT 2 in the log). I published
   by copying a scratchpad render and ran G7 immediately after; **G7 should be re-run on the final
   artifact after any later pack run.**
5. Everything else from E-1 §6 / E-2 §7 stands: door leaves hidden not swung, hardware GL assumed,
   the driver muxes the mp4, `out/walkthrough.json` read by no gate.
