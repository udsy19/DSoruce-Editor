## 2. How the camera path is built

### 2.1 The circulation field

`crates/ds-core/src/circulation.rs` already builds precisely what a camera needs — an occupancy grid
plus a distance transform — but **`Editor.circulation()` only returns scores across the wasm
boundary**: no grid, no skeleton, no centrelines. `crates/ds-core/**` is B1's lane this cycle, so
rather than add an export I rebuilt the equivalent field in TS, seeded from `circulation()`'s own
`cell_size` (0.15 m here) so the camera's idea of a corridor and the *scored* one are the same
resolution. `circulation()` is also the guard: the take refuses to run on a document with no walls.

`buildClearanceGrid()` rasterises walls at true thickness, every non-door component footprint
(rotated), the service core, and everything outside the plate; then **doors re-carve their own
opening** — without that no route reaches any room. The distance transform is exact (Felzenszwalb &
Huttenlocher), not a chamfer approximation.

### 2.2 The route

`routeBetween()` is an 8-connected Dijkstra whose per-cell cost rises quadratically as clearance
drops below 1.15 m. That cost is the whole point: it puts the path on the corridor **centreline**
instead of scraping doorframes, and it is what makes "circulation-graph waypoints" a real thing
rather than a phrase. Legs are concatenated, RDP-simplified, run through a centripetal
`THREE.CatmullRomCurve3`, re-seated on the clearance field (a spline overshoot on a tight corner can
bulge into a partition) and lightly relaxed under a clearance guard.

`planWalkRoute()` resolves the brief's route against whatever the document contains — outside
reception → main corridor → out into the open floor → back to the conference room → hero wide — and
every stop is snapped to the best free point near its ideal position. Resolved for the demo plan:

```
outside-reception (2.4, 5.2)   →  corridor (12.1, 5.1)   →  open-space (15.9, 11.2)
  →  open-space-far (24.6, 10.8)  →  conference-threshold (31.2, 5.1)  →  conference-hero (30.1, 3.4)
51.48 m · 1.226 m/s · 8 threshold passes
```

### 2.3 Poses

Speed is integrated forward at 1/fps with (a) a per-arc-length multiplier that **slows to 0.5× within
1.5 m of any door** and at authored stops, and (b) an ease-in/ease-out envelope; the base speed is
bisected so the walk lands exactly at the end of the path. The last second is a **settle** — the
camera is already parked at the hero — because the yaw smoother averages over ±0.75 s and without it
the video ends half-way through the final turn (measured: the hero's branded screen was delivered
cut off at the frame edge).

Yaw is a weighted blend of a 7.5 m look-ahead and the authored look targets, unwrapped and
Gaussian-smoothed. Pitch is locked at 0 and eye height at 1.60 m, per `video-spec.json`.
Measured at full frame rate: **mean 11.1 °/s, peak 45.4 °/s** — the peak is the right-angle turn off
the corridor, which cannot be taken at walking pace for less.

### 2.4 Two things that would have put the camera inside geometry

1. **The "am I inside a solid?" test.** Exact world-AABB containment, never ray parity — D's
   correction, reused rather than re-derived. Every pose is checked; the run reports
   **`containmentFixes: 0`, `minClearanceM: 0.41`**.
2. **Closed door leaves.** `furniture3d.buildDoor` models a *shut* door — a solid slab filling the
   opening. The wall spans already have the opening cut out, so the leaf is the only obstacle, and
   the camera would have walked straight through 5 of them. `openDoorways()` hides the leaf slab
   (17 doors) and keeps the dark-metal jambs and head, so a threshold still reads as a doorway.
   *I first implemented this as a real 168° swing, measured it, and threw it away*: folded flat, a
   leaf lines the wall the camera turns toward, and it put a full-frame slab of oak in the hero shot
   and in two corridor passes; at 90° it stands 0.9 m into a route the router has already committed
   to. Doors remain in the document, the plan and the takeoff — this is a camera convention, not a
   change of quantity. (The initial `containmentFixes: 1` was a *hidden* leaf still being counted as
   a solid; `solidBoxes` now skips invisible meshes.)

## 3. Branding

* **In-scene** — three wall-mounted displays carrying `renderBrandMarkPng()`, unlit
  (`MeshBasicMaterial`, which is what a powered screen looks like next to tone-mapped plasterboard):
  the reception's feature wall `(0.9, 2.5)`, the conference room's identity wall `(25.9, 2.1)`, and
  the service core's flank facing the open floor `(17, 12)`. They are hung **after** the route is
  planned, on walls resolved by the *same* `facingWall()` call the hero pose aims at, so the final
  frame is guaranteed to contain the mark. (A first version hung them in "the largest conference
  room", which on a plan with four identical ones is a *different* room from the one the camera
  visits — the hero shot came back branding-free.)
* **Title card** — 1.5 s, cross-dissolving over 0.5 s into live footage (the camera is already
  walking as the card clears). This is the ruled-in divergence from qbiq, who brand in-scene only.

## 5. Honest visual verdict — I watched my own output

I extracted and read frames at every iteration, not just the statistics, and the video changed
substantially because of it. Three defects that G7 passed straight through and only my eyes caught:

1. **The camera walked through five closed doors** (§2.4). Statistically invisible.
2. **The hero shot's branded screen was in the wrong room** (§3), so the take ended on blank
   plasterboard.
3. **The screens were buried inside their own walls** — a zone edge is the wall *centreline*, so a
   50 mm standoff on a 200 mm partition puts the display inside the wall. Nothing rendered at all.

**What the finished take is.** Title card → the reception, read from the circulation side through its
glazed front (sage feature wall, both black ring luminaires, oak table) → the main corridor with the
perforated-bronze screen on the right and a vanishing point down the spine → off the corridor into
the open floor → **the core's DSOURCE display, large in frame, with the desk banks and perimeter
glazing behind it** → four sustained open-plan runs (bench desks receding, black linear pendants, the
downlight grid, daylight along the whole facade) → back up past a phone-booth pod to the conference
room → in through the threshold → ending on the meeting table with the branded screen on the wall
behind it. It reads as one continuous walk through a coherent building.

**Versus `docs/reference/qbiq/spec/video-frames/`.** The reference is better, and by a knowable
margin.

* *Motion and grammar:* comparable, and deliberately so. Both are single unbroken takes at a locked
  1.6 m eye height with zero pitch and no shake. The reference's yaw is a lazier 4–6 °/s over a
  shorter path; mine averages 11 °/s and peaks at 45 °/s on the corner off the corridor. Mine is more
  "walking briskly through a building", theirs is more "gimbal on a slider".
* *Content:* the reference's rooms are *dressed* — a fluted reception desk, tub chairs and a patterned
  rug, planting, sculpted ring luminaires, a perforated bronze library wall, exposed services and
  cable baskets overhead, iMacs and Aeron chairs, ottomans, a bar-height collaboration table, and a
  photoreal city beyond the glass. Ours has bench desks, monitors, task chairs, oak doors, one bronze
  screen, one sage wall, a flat ceiling with a downlight grid, and a green horizon band. Room for room
  it is the same *plan*, at maybe 55–60 % of the *dressing*.
* *Light:* the reference holds a very tight 0.61–0.67 mean luminance across all ten samples. Ours
  swings 0.55–0.82 — measurably wider, because a plan whose partitions are all white plasterboard
  throws far more light back than one lined in timber and bronze. Our exposure never changes; the
  scene does.
* *Composition:* the reference always keeps a strong near-field anchor (millwork, a planter, a desk
  return) on one edge. Ours does at the reception, the bronze screen, the core display and the hero,
  but **at two of the twelve sampled moments — the turn off the corridor (~t=11 s) and the climb back
  out of the open floor (~t=31 s) — the frame is more than half blank white partition.** That is the
  honest weak spot of this take and I am not hiding it: on this plan there is nothing else to look at
  at those two instants.

**What I did not do:** no post filter, no grade, no vignette, no bloom pass laid over a flat image,
no sharpening. Every improvement in this report is a change to the camera, the route or the scene.

## 6. Open risks

1. **The last frame's luminance has only 0.034 of margin.** G7 samples `duration − 0.3 s`, which lands
   on the hero: measured mean 0.816 against a ceiling of 0.85. It is deterministic for this artifact,
   but a whiter room on another document could cross it. **I deliberately did not lower the exposure
   to buy margin** — D-1 §2 requires the video to use exposure 1.15 / env 0.42 verbatim, and dimming
   only the video would make it a different building from the stills. The right fix is a
   scene/palette change agreed with D, not a per-deliverable exposure.
2. **1.23 m/s, against the spec's 0.8.** `target.camera.speed_mps` is a note, not a G7 assertion, and
   the duration ceiling is the binding constraint: 51.5 m of route inside 43 s. The route is that long
   because entering the open floor and coming back out is worth ~25 s of the best footage in the take
   — with a single shallow open-space stop the route was 35.8 m at 0.94 m/s and the open plan was only
   ever seen side-on down a corridor. If a slower dolly matters more than the open-plan run, move the
   two `dip()` calls in `planWalkRoute` back to one shallow stop.
3. **Two blank-partition moments** (§5). Fixable only by dressing the model (bulkheads, signage,
   planting, an accent wall on the support-room flanks) — i.e. a scene change in D's lane, not a
   camera change in mine.
4. **Door leaves are hidden, not swung.** Thresholds read as cased openings with a frame. Deliberate
   (§2.4) and documented, but it is a visible difference from the stills, where doors are closed.
5. **Hardware GL.** The harness launches Chromium with `--use-angle=metal` and gets a real M4 Pro
   Metal context; `softwareGL: false`. On a SwiftShader-only host the renderer drops GTAO (the same
   guard `Viewer3D` and `interiorStill` use) and the take will render flatter. I have **not** verified
   G7's luminance band there — and given risk 1's thin margin, a flatter render is more likely to pass,
   not less.
6. **The mp4 is muxed by the driver, not the module.** `renderWalkthrough` is medium-agnostic: it
   hands out one canvas per frame and the driver encodes. That keeps the one export action honest
   headlessly (ffmpeg on PATH), but an in-browser "Export video" button would need a wasm encoder or a
   `MediaRecorder` path — not built, and not in this deliverable's scope.
7. **`out/walkthrough.json` is mine alone** and is not read by any gate. It carries the route, the
   stop list, the 84-sample camera track, display positions and the clearance/yaw statistics — it is
   the evidence behind every number in this report, and the first place to look if a future plan
   routes badly.
