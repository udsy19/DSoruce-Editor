Four assertions per case (`seeded` + `dwg` — the dwg pack for its 162-instance desk field, the case
that forced the `placeNear` change):

1. **Structure** — 11 sheets; each sheet banner present the exact number of times it should be
   (`SECTIONS` twice, everything else once).
2. **Determinism** — a second, independent render of the same document is **byte-identical**. This is
   G4's determinism twin, applied to the path G4 never looks at, and it is what covers the embedded
   rasters (see below).
3. **Rooms + leaders** — for every non-circulation zone *the core places*, its name appears on A.01

   end on the label box and the other inside that room's footprint**.

### Why this satisfies `.claude/rules/gate-independence.md`

* **Re-derived from the artifact bytes.** The reader parses the PDF itself — `pdf.ts` keeps content
  streams uncompressed, one op per line, so every text/line/rect/image op is read off the wire. No
  `Page.ops` object, no producer summary, is opened. An op the reader does not recognise is a
  **failure**, not a skip, so a new drawing primitive cannot slip past unmeasured.
* **The expected side is the core state**, not the producer: the room set and geometry come from
  re-running the Rust generator in Node (`demo-doc.mjs`), projected through a world→sheet transform
  the test **recomputes itself** (a deliberate second derivation of `pdf.ts`'s fit + `planBox`, in the
  same spirit as G11's Python copy). If the renderer's fit and the test's diverge, the room checks
  fail loudly instead of measuring nothing.
* **A missing baseline FAILS.** No fixture, no pass:
  ```
  $ node scripts/drawing-set.test.mjs        # with the fixture removed
  FAIL: no baseline at scripts/fixtures/drawing-set.baseline.json.
  The drawing set is unprotected without one. Render the set, LOOK at every sheet, then
  record it with: node scripts/drawing-set.test.mjs --update
  ```
* **Two things are deliberately excluded from the digest, and each is covered another way.** The
  title-block **date** (the one value that legitimately differs between runs) is masked to `<DATE>`.
  The embedded **JPEG bytes** are not hashed — that is a Chromium encoder detail that would turn the
  fixture red on a browser upgrade for no drawing change; the rasters are covered by assertion 2
  (byte-identical twin renders) and by their placement + pixel dimensions, which *are* digested.
* **The baseline was recorded from an inspected render.** The 22 images in §2 are that render. I
  looked at every sheet of both packs before `--update`, and again after (the code is unchanged
  between the two: the test passes against the recorded fixture at 252 checks).

## 4. Leader lines — the threshold, and what it actually does

Shared machinery, in `sheetSet.ts` next to `placeNear`, used by **both** consumers:

```ts
export function labelLeader(anchorX, anchorY, pos, w, h, room?): Leader | null
export function zoneBoxOnSheet(shape, map): OccBox
```

`placeNear` returns a point; `labelLeader` turns "where it ended up" into "does it still say which
room it names". Each consumer draws the returned segment with its own primitive — `p.line(…, gray
0.5, width 0.4)` on the sheets (matching the existing opening-tag leader), a 1 px stroke in
`palette.plan.linework` on the canvas, drawn **before** that label's halo, so legibility is
unchanged.

### The rule

> **A leader is drawn when the label's box no longer touches its room's footprint** — and the line
> runs from the label's edge to the **room's edge**, never across it.

Justification, in the order that decided it:

1. **It is the ambiguity condition itself, not a proxy.** While the label overlaps the room it names,
   it *is* the attachment; the moment it clears it, nothing says which room it belongs to. No px
   threshold has to be invented.
2. **It scales with the drawing, which a fixed distance cannot.** A 668 m² open workspace absorbs a
   large nudge and stays unambiguous; a 1.4 m² booth is smaller than its own label, so a clear
   displacement escapes it entirely. Same rule, both cases.
3. **It cannot fire for a label nobody moved** — `placeNear`'s first candidate is the true centre,
   which is inside the room by construction.
4. **Stopping at the room's edge is what keeps E7 closed.** A leader that ran to the room *centre*
   would cross the very chair the label was moved off. Measured cost of the edge rule below: 2 px.
5. `MIN_LEADER = 6` (pt on a sheet, px on the canvas) drops sub-tick lines: below that the label is
   touching the room anyway and the line would be noise, not a pointer.

Rooms with no single footprint (the plan's aggregated circulation label, Room ID `0`) fall back to
"displaced beyond its own label box".

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

on **dwg A.01** — a sheet where the rooms are not drawn at all, so the leader is the only thing

opening-tag leaders in the *same* hairline grey, so counting lines by eye over-reports; the numbers
here are the test's attribution — label box at one end, that room's footprint at the other.)

### Rooms 155 / 163 / 171 — what the crops actually show

The brief's premise does not survive measurement, and I am not going to draw a line to pretend it
does. On `out/plan.png`, all three booth labels are displaced by **exactly one candidate step**
(`dv = h + 3 = 24 px`) and their label boxes still **overlap their booths by 2.0 px**; the digits
themselves clear the booth wall by **3 px** and sit horizontally **centred on their own booth**
(155 below, 163 above, 171 below). Measured, that is a label touching its room, not floating between
two. A leader there would be a 1 px stub — invisible at print size and pure noise on the plate. So:

* **before / after these crops are byte-identical**, and so is the whole file:
  `md5 out/plan.png = 4223bea02ff4f6d7b56a6895c09f8aa6` before and after my change. The delivered
  pack is untouched.
* the machinery is in place and shared, and it **does** fire the moment a booth label goes one step
  further — which is exactly what happens on the dwg plan (rooms 214 and 247, crops below) and on 20
  labels across the four plan sheets of the two drawing sets.

I would rather ship a rule that is right about when a leader is needed than a leader on every
one-step nudge. The test in §3 makes the rule enforceable either way: raise or lower the threshold
and the baseline goes red, on purpose.

### Cost, in ink — the G11 question, with numbers

The dwg plan graphic is the only delivered raster my change touches: **93 pixels differ** in the whole
1040×780 plate (two leaders). Of those 93, **2 pixels** fall inside one desk's bounding box — the
`247` leader grazes a corner — worst case **−0.02** on that instance's `ink_per_outline`. G11's
statistics are unchanged:

```
seeded   min=1.19 p25=1.48 median=1.73   G11 PASS (56 checks)   ← identical to Q-1
dwg      min=0.99 p25=1.39 median=1.48   G11 PASS (51 checks)   ← identical to Q-1
testfit  min=1.29 p25=1.50 median=2.06   G11 PASS (62 checks)   ← identical to Q-1
```

The leader is also *pure-neutral* ink (`palette.plan.linework` = `#000000`), so G11 classes it as
label ink, never as furniture — it can only ever lower the measure, never inflate it. That is the
safe direction: if a future leader did cross a symbol, G11 would say so.
